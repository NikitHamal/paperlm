## Group B: written

11. Two large parallel metal plates carry surface charge densities $+3.54\times10^{-6}\ \text{C m}^{-2}$ and $-3.54\times10^{-6}\ \text{C m}^{-2}$. Find the field between the plates and the force on an electron placed there. [2]

12. Explain the relationship between molar heat capacities $C_v$ and $C_p$ considering the work done by an ideal gas at constant pressure and constant volume. [3]

13. Explain how the position of an object relative to the focal point ($F$) and center of curvature ($C$) affects the type of image formed by a concave mirror, considering both real and virtual images. Use the mirror formula to support your explanation. [5]

14. Using the provided formula, calculate the temperature $T$ using Wien's displacement law if $\lambda_m = 500 \times 10^{-9}$ m. Additionally, explain how Searle's method and Lee's disc are used to measure the thermal conductivity $k$ for good and bad conductors, respectively. [5]

## Answer key and marking scheme

1. (a) $5\times10^{-4}$ - The strain is calculated as the stress divided by the Young's modulus, which equals $5\times10^{-4}$.
2. (c) $\text{C V}^{-1}$ - from $C = Q/V$, coulomb per volt.
3. (d) $931.5\ \text{MeV}$ - from $E = mc^{2}$ with $m = 1.66\times10^{-27}\ \text{kg}$.
4. (b) $30\ \text{cm}$ in front - with $u = -15\ \text{cm}$, $f = -10\ \text{cm}$: $1/v = 1/f - 1/u = -1/10 + 1/15 = -1/30$, so $v = -30\ \text{cm}$, i.e. $30\ \text{cm}$ in front of the mirror and real.
5. (a) no unit - it is a ratio of two angles (or of two refractive-index differences).
6. (b) Newton's law of cooling - The equation directly corresponds to Newton's law of cooling as provided.
7. (c) the $60\ \text{W}$ bulb is brighter - same current in series, and $P = I^{2}R$ with $R_{60} > R_{100}$.
8. (c) frequency - the source sets the frequency; the medium sets the speed, and therefore the wavelength through $v = f\lambda$.
9. (a) $\vec{J} = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \vec{F}\,dt = \Delta \vec{p}$ - The impulse of a force equals the change in momentum it produces.
10. (d) $0.1\ \text{mm}$ - $10\ \text{VSD} = 9\ \text{MSD}$, so $1\ \text{VSD} = 0.9\ \text{mm}$ and $\text{L.C.} = 1 - 0.9 = 0.1\ \text{mm}$. Option(b) is the screw-gauge least count, quoted from memory.
11. [2 marks] Between the plates, $E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0 = 3.54\times10^{-6}/8.85\times10^{-12} = 4.0\times10^{5}\ \text{V m}^{-1}$, directed from the positive to the negative plate. [2]
12. [3 marks] At const. pressure, extra heat does work against surroundings. [1] | For a small temp. rise dT, heat supplied equals work plus internal energy change. [1] | At const. volume, no work is done, so heat equals internal energy change, defining $C_v$. [1]
13. [5 marks] The position of an object relative to $F$ and $C$ on a concave mirror determines the image type. [1] | When the object is beyond $C$, a real, inverted, and diminished image forms between $F$ and $C$. [1] | Between $C$ and $F$, the image is real, inverted, and magnified. [1] | At $F$, no image is formed as rays are parallel. [1] | Between $F$ and the pole, a virtual, upright, and magnified image is formed. [1]
14. [5 marks] From Wien's displacement law, $T = \frac{b}{\lambda_m} = \frac{2.898 \times 10^{-3}}{500 \times 10^{-9}} = 5.80 \times 10^3\ \text{K}$. [2] | Searle's method calculates $k$ for good conductors using $k = \frac{mc_w(\theta_4 - \theta_3)x}{A(\theta_1 - \theta_2)t}$. [1] | Lee's disc method is employed for poor conductors, offering an alternative measurement technique. [1] | Both methods aim to determine thermal conductivity but apply different setups based on material properties. [1]
