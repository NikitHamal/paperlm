## Answer key and marking scheme

1. (a) Ni²⁺ - Ni²⁺ is 3d⁸, partly filled, and absorbs in the red, appearing green. Zn²⁺, Sc³⁺ and Ag⁺ are all d¹⁰ or d⁰ and colourless.
2. (c) m-nitrobenzoic acid - the carboxyl group withdraws electrons, deactivating the ring and directing meta.
3. (d) C₃S - tricalcium silicate hydrates within about a week; C₂S continues for months and supplies the final strength.
4. (b) o- and p-nitrochlorobenzene - chlorine deactivates the ring but still directs ortho and para through its lone pair; the para isomer predominates.
5. (a) a protective oxide layer forms - the acid oxidises the surface to a thin, coherent film of Fe₃O₄ which seals the metal; this is why conc. HNO₃ can be carried in iron containers.
6. (b) +7 - all five 3d and both 4s electrons are used, as in MnO₄⁻.
7. (c) control the reaction and obtain the p-isomer - acetylation reduces the activating power of the nitrogen and blocks oxidation by the nitrating mixture, so the para product can be isolated.
8. (c) colourless - the tertiary nitroalkane has no α-hydrogen, so it cannot form the nitrolic acid or pseudonitrole that give the colours.
9. (a) increases - Ostwald's law, $\alpha = \sqrt{K/C}$, and $C$ falls on dilution.
10. (d) methyl orange - Na₂CO₃ is a weak base; at complete neutralisation the solution holds H₂CO₃ (pH about 4), which methyl orange signals. Phenolphthalein changes colour at the half-way stage, NaHCO₃, so it would give a wrong end point.
11. [2 marks] In 2-bromo-2-methylpropane the halogen-bearing carbon carries three methyl groups. [2]
12. [2 marks] (i) 2CHCl₃ + O₂ → 2COCl₂ + 2HCl (phosgene). [1] | (ii) 2CHCl₃ + 6Ag → C₂H₂ + 6AgCl (ethyne). [1]
