## bio12: 10 numeric templates

- In a double-stranded DNA sample adenine makes up $26\%$ of the bases. What percentage is guanine?
  (a) $24\ \%$  (b) $48\ \%$  (c) $26\ \%$  (d) $74\ \%$
  Key: (a). Chargaff's rules: $A = T$ and $G = C$, so $2A + 2G = 100\%$. $T = A = 26\%$, so $G + C = 100 - 2\times 26$ $G = \dfrac{100 - 2A}{2} = 24\%$

- A DNA fragment has $40$ A-T base pairs and $30$ G-C base pairs. How many hydrogen bonds hold the two strands together?
  (a) $140$  (b) $170$  (c) $70$  (d) $180$
  Key: (b). A-T pairs are joined by 2 hydrogen bonds and G-C pairs by 3. $2\times 40 + 3\times 30$ Total $= 170$

- A monohybrid cross $Tt \times Tt$ produces $320$ offspring. How many are expected to show the dominant phenotype?
  (a) $240$  (b) $180$  (c) $160$  (d) $80$
  Key: (a). The F2 phenotypic ratio of a monohybrid cross is $3:1$. Dominant fraction $= \dfrac{3}{4}$ Number $= \dfrac{3}{4}\times 320 = 240$

- A dihybrid cross $AaBb \times AaBb$ gives $480$ offspring. How many are expected to show both dominant traits?
  (a) $270$  (b) $360$  (c) $30$  (d) $90$
  Key: (a). The dihybrid F2 phenotypic ratio is $9:3:3:1$; both dominant traits form $9/16$. Double-dominant fraction $= \dfrac{9}{16}$ Number $= \dfrac{9}{16}\times 480 = 270$

- A test cross gives $45$ recombinant offspring out of $500$. Find the recombination frequency.
  (a) $9.89\ \%$  (b) $9\ \%$  (c) $91\ \%$  (d) $0.09\ \%$
  Key: (b). Recombination frequency $= \dfrac{\text{recombinants}}{\text{total offspring}}\times 100$. $RF = \dfrac{45}{500}\times 100$ $RF = 9\%$

- A PCR starts with one DNA molecule. How many copies exist after $10$ cycles?
  (a) $2048$  (b) $1024$  (c) $100$  (d) $20$
  Key: (b). Each PCR cycle doubles the DNA, so copies $=2^n$. Copies $= 2^{10}$ Copies $= 1024$

- A person has a heart rate of $72\ \text{beats min}^{-1}$ and a stroke volume of $70\ \text{mL}$. What is the cardiac output in litres per minute?
  (a) $5.04\ \text{L min}^{-1}$  (b) $50.4\ \text{L min}^{-1}$  (c) $10.1\ \text{L min}^{-1}$  (d) $1.03\ \text{L min}^{-1}$
  Key: (a). Cardiac output $=$ heart rate $\times$ stroke volume. $CO = 72\times 70\ \text{mL min}^{-1}$ $CO = 5.04\ \text{L min}^{-1}$

- A plant cell has a solute potential $\Psi_s = -1\ \text{MPa}$ and a pressure potential $\Psi_p = +0.4\ \text{MPa}$. Find its water potential $\Psi_w$.
  (a) $-1\ \text{MPa}$  (b) $-1.4\ \text{MPa}$  (c) $-0.6\ \text{MPa}$  (d) $-1.8\ \text{MPa}$
  Key: (c). Water potential $\Psi_w = \Psi_s + \Psi_p$. $\Psi_w = -1 + 0.4$ $\Psi_w = -0.6\ \text{MPa}$

- Glycolysis breaks down $4$ glucose molecules. What is the net gain of ATP?
  (a) $8$  (b) $152$  (c) $4$  (d) $16$
  Key: (a). Glycolysis yields a net gain of 2 ATP per glucose (4 produced, 2 used). Net ATP $= 2\times 4$ Net ATP $= 8$

- A town of $40000$ people records $1000$ births in a year. Find the crude birth rate per 1000 people.
  (a) $25\ \text{per 1000}$  (b) $50\ \text{per 1000}$  (c) $2.5\ \text{per 1000}$  (d) $12.5\ \text{per 1000}$
  Key: (a). Crude birth rate $=$ (births / mid-year population) $\times 1000$. CBR $= \dfrac{1000}{40000}\times 1000$ CBR $= 25$ per 1000

## phy11: 36 numeric templates

- A car moving with an initial velocity of $8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ accelerates uniformly at $6\ \text{m s}^{-2}$ for $12\ \text{s}$. Find its final velocity.
  (a) $96\ \text{m s}^{-1}$  (b) $40\ \text{m s}^{-1}$  (c) $14\ \text{m s}^{-1}$  (d) $80\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
  Key: (d). For uniform acceleration the first equation of motion is $v = u + at$. $v = u + at = 8 + 6\times 12$ $v = 80\ \text{m s}^{-1}$

- A particle starts with a velocity of $7\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ and accelerates uniformly at $5\ \text{m s}^{-2}$. Calculate the distance travelled in $10\ \text{s}$.
  (a) $250\ \text{m}$  (b) $570\ \text{m}$  (c) $320\ \text{m}$  (d) $95\ \text{m}$
  Key: (c). For uniform acceleration the second equation of motion is $s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^{2}$. $s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^{2} = 7\times10 + \tfrac{1}{2}\times5\times10^{2}$ $s = 320\ \text{m}$

- A vehicle covers a distance at $30\ \text{km h}^{-1}$ and returns the same distance at $80\ \text{km h}^{-1}$. Find the average speed for the whole journey.
  (a) $21.8\ \text{km h}^{-1}$  (b) $55\ \text{km h}^{-1}$  (c) $43.6\ \text{km h}^{-1}$  (d) $49\ \text{km h}^{-1}$
  Key: (c). For equal distances the average speed is $\dfrac{2v_1v_2}{v_1+v_2}$ (total distance over total time). $v_{av} = \dfrac{2v_1v_2}{v_1+v_2} = \dfrac{2\times 30\times 80}{30+80}$ $v_{av} = 43.6\ \text{km h}^{-1}$

- A projectile is launched with a speed of $30\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ at an angle of $45^{\circ}$ above the horizontal on level ground. Find its horizontal range. (Take $g = 9.8\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.)
  (a) $45.9\ \text{m}$  (b) $91.8\ \text{m}$  (c) $3.06\ \text{m}$  (d) $64.9\ \text{m}$
  Key: (b). The horizontal range of a projectile on level ground is $R = \dfrac{u^{2}\sin 2\theta}{g}$. $R = \dfrac{u^{2}\sin 2\theta}{g} = \dfrac{30^{2}\sin(2\times45^{\circ})}{9.8}$ $R = 91.8\ \text{m}$

- A projectile is thrown with speed $30\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ at $60^{\circ}$ to the horizontal. Taking $g = 10\ \text{m s}^{-2}$, find the maximum height reached.
  (a) $45\ \text{m}$  (b) $39\ \text{m}$  (c) $33.7\ \text{m}$  (d) $67.5\ \text{m}$
  Key: (c). Maximum height is $H = \dfrac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}$. $H = \dfrac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g} = \dfrac{(30)^2\sin^2 60^{\circ}}{2\times10}$ $H = 33.7\ \text{m}$

- A force acts on a body of mass $20\ \text{kg}$ and produces an acceleration of $7\ \text{m s}^{-2}$. Calculate the magnitude of the force.
  (a) $140\ \text{N}$  (b) $27\ \text{N}$  (c) $280\ \text{N}$  (d) $2.86\ \text{N}$
  Key: (a). Newton's second law states that the net force equals mass times acceleration, $F = ma$. $F = ma = 20\times7$ $F = 140\ \text{N}$

- A body of mass $5\ \text{kg}$ changes its velocity from $6\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ to $30\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ in $3\ \text{s}$. Find the average force acting on it.
  (a) $40\ \text{N}$  (b) $120\ \text{N}$  (c) $8\ \text{N}$  (d) $60\ \text{N}$
  Key: (a). Force equals the rate of change of momentum, $F = \dfrac{m(v-u)}{t}$. $F = \dfrac{m(v-u)}{t} = \dfrac{5(30-6)}{3}$ $F = 40\ \text{N}$

- Find the gravitational potential energy of a body of mass $10\ \text{kg}$ raised to a height of $18\ \text{m}$ above the ground. (Take $g = 9.8\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.)
  (a) $882\ \text{J}$  (b) $1764\ \text{J}$  (c) $98\ \text{J}$  (d) $180\ \text{J}$
  Key: (b). Gravitational potential energy near the Earth's surface is $U = mgh$. $U = mgh = 10\times 9.8\times18$ $U = 1764\ \text{J}$

- Calculate the kinetic energy of a body of mass $10\ \text{kg}$ moving with a speed of $12\ \text{m s}^{-1}$.
  (a) $60\ \text{J}$  (b) $1440\ \text{J}$  (c) $720\ \text{J}$  (d) $120\ \text{J}$
  Key: (c). The kinetic energy of a body of mass $m$ moving with speed $v$ is $K = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^{2}$. $K = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}\times10\times12^{2}$ $K = 720\ \text{J}$

- A force of $50\ \text{N}$ acts on a body at an angle of $60^{\circ}$ to the direction of motion and displaces it by $12\ \text{m}$. Calculate the work done.
  (a) $520\ \text{J}$  (b) $600\ \text{J}$  (c) $300\ \text{J}$  (d) $150\ \text{J}$
  Key: (c). The work done by a constant force is $W = Fs\cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between force and displacement. $W = Fs\cos\theta = 50\times12\times\cos 60^{\circ}$ $W = 300\ \text{J}$

- A machine does $3000\ \text{J}$ of work in $40\ \text{s}$. What is its power output?
  (a) $75\ \text{W}$  (b) $150\ \text{W}$  (c) $37.5\ \text{W}$  (d) $750\ \text{W}$
  Key: (a). Power is the rate of doing work, $P = \dfrac{W}{t}$. $P = \dfrac{W}{t} = \dfrac{3000}{40}$ $P = 75\ \text{W}$

- A body of mass $5\ \text{kg}$ moves in a circle of radius $10\ \text{m}$ with a uniform speed of $12\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. Find the centripetal force.
  (a) $7200\ \text{N}$  (b) $6\ \text{N}$  (c) $72\ \text{N}$  (d) $36\ \text{N}$
  Key: (c). The centripetal force on a body of mass $m$ moving with speed $v$ in a circle of radius $r$ is $F = \dfrac{mv^{2}}{r}$. $F = \dfrac{mv^{2}}{r} = \dfrac{5\times12^{2}}{10}$ $F = 72\ \text{N}$

- A force of $5000\ \text{N}$ is applied normally to a cross-section of area $12\ \text{cm}^{2}$. Calculate the stress in MPa.
  (a) $4.17\ \text{MPa}$  (b) $0.417\ \text{MPa}$  (c) $8.34\ \text{MPa}$  (d) $417\ \text{MPa}$
  Key: (a). Stress is the restoring force per unit area, $\sigma = \dfrac{F}{A}$. $\sigma = \dfrac{F}{A} = \dfrac{5000}{12\times10^{-4}}\ \text{Pa}$ $\sigma = 4.17\ \text{MPa}$

- A steel bar under a stress of $200\ \text{MPa}$ shows a strain of $0.001$. Find Young's modulus.
  (a) $200\ \text{GPa}$  (b) $20\ \text{GPa}$  (c) $400\ \text{GPa}$  (d) $2000\ \text{GPa}$
  Key: (a). Young's modulus is $E = \dfrac{\text{stress}}{\text{strain}}$. $E = \dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon} = \dfrac{200\ \text{MPa}}{0.001}$ $E = 200\ \text{GPa}$

- How much heat is required to raise the temperature of $300\ \text{g}$ of water by $30\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$? (Specific heat capacity of water $= 4.2\ \text{J g}^{-1}\,^{\circ}\text{C}^{-1}$.)
  (a) $37800\ \text{J}$  (b) $75600\ \text{J}$  (c) $9000\ \text{J}$  (d) $18900\ \text{J}$
  Key: (a). The heat absorbed is $Q = mc\Delta T$. $Q = mc\Delta T = 300\times4.2\times30$ $Q = 37800\ \text{J}$

- How much heat is needed to change $0.5\ \text{kg}$ of a substance having specific latent heat $2260\ \text{kJ kg}^{-1}$ at its transition temperature?
  (a) $2260\ \text{kJ}$  (b) $1130\ \text{kJ}$  (c) $2261\ \text{kJ}$  (d) $4520\ \text{kJ}$
  Key: (b). Heat absorbed during a change of state is $Q = mL$. $Q = mL = 0.5\times 2260$ $Q = 1130\ \text{kJ}$

- A rod of length $2\ \text{m}$ has a coefficient of linear expansion $19\times10^{-6}\ \text{K}^{-1}$. By how many millimetres does it expand when heated by $100\ \text{K}$?
  (a) $0.2\ \text{mm}$  (b) $1.9\ \text{mm}$  (c) $38\ \text{mm}$  (d) $3.8\ \text{mm}$
  Key: (d). Linear expansion is $\Delta L = L\alpha\,\Delta T$. $\Delta L = L\alpha\Delta T = 2\times 19\times10^{-6}\times 100\ \text{m}$ $\Delta L = 3.8\ \text{mm}$

- Find the pressure of $3\ \text{mol}$ of an ideal gas confined in $50\ \text{L}$ at $350\ \text{K}$. (Take $R = 8.314\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$.)
  (a) $350\ \text{kPa}$  (b) $175\ \text{kPa}$  (c) $87.3\ \text{kPa}$  (d) $21\ \text{kPa}$
  Key: (b). For an ideal gas $PV = nRT$. $P = \dfrac{nRT}{V} = \dfrac{3\times8.314\times350}{50}$ $P = 175\ \text{kPa}$

- How many moles of an ideal gas occupy $20\ \text{L}$ at $2\ \text{atm}$ and $400\ \text{K}$? ($R = 0.0821\ \text{L atm K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$)
  (a) $2.44\ \text{mol}$  (b) $1.22\ \text{mol}$  (c) $0.821\ \text{mol}$  (d) $0.1\ \text{mol}$
  Key: (b). The ideal gas equation is $PV = nRT$, so $n = \dfrac{PV}{RT}$. $n = \dfrac{PV}{RT} = \dfrac{2\times 20}{0.0821\times 400}$ $n = 1.22\ \text{mol}$

- A gas occupies $6\ \text{L}$ at $3\ \text{atm}$. What volume will it occupy at $8\ \text{atm}$ if the temperature is constant?
  (a) $1.13\ \text{L}$  (b) $66\ \text{L}$  (c) $16\ \text{L}$  (d) $2.25\ \text{L}$
  Key: (d). Boyle's law states that at constant temperature $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$. $V_2 = \dfrac{P_1V_1}{P_2} = \dfrac{3\times6}{8}$ $V_2 = 2.25\ \text{L}$

- A gas occupies $6\ \text{L}$ at $300\ \text{K}$. Find its volume at $500\ \text{K}$ at constant pressure.
  (a) $206\ \text{L}$  (b) $3.6\ \text{L}$  (c) $10\ \text{L}$  (d) $5\ \text{L}$
  Key: (c). Charles' law states that at constant pressure $\dfrac{V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{V_2}{T_2}$ (T in kelvin). $V_2 = V_1\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = 6\times\dfrac{500}{300}$ $V_2 = 10\ \text{L}$

- An object is placed $40\ \text{cm}$ from a concave mirror of focal length $10\ \text{cm}$. Find the distance of the real image from the mirror.
  (a) $30\ \text{cm}$  (b) $8\ \text{cm}$  (c) $13.3\ \text{cm}$  (d) $50\ \text{cm}$
  Key: (c). The mirror formula (real-is-positive convention) is $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v}$. $\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{1}{40}$ $v = 13.3\ \text{cm}$

- A ray of light incident at $45^{\circ}$ in air is refracted at $25^{\circ}$ in a medium. Find the refractive index of the medium.
  (a) $1.8$  (b) $0.598$  (c) $1.67$  (d) $0.78$
  Key: (c). Snell's law gives the refractive index as $n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$. $n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \dfrac{\sin 45^{\circ}}{\sin 25^{\circ}}$ $n = 1.67$

- The speed of light in a medium is $1.5\times10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. Taking $c = 3\times10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, find the refractive index of the medium.
  (a) $2$  (b) $1.5$  (c) $4.5$  (d) $0.5$
  Key: (a). Refractive index is $n = \dfrac{c}{v}$. $n = \dfrac{c}{v} = \dfrac{3\times10^{8}}{1.5\times10^{8}}$ $n = 2$

- An object is placed $45\ \text{cm}$ in front of a convex lens of focal length $15\ \text{cm}$. Find the distance of the real image from the lens.
  (a) $60\ \text{cm}$  (b) $22.5\ \text{cm}$  (c) $30\ \text{cm}$  (d) $11.3\ \text{cm}$
  Key: (b). The lens formula (Cartesian sign convention) is $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u}$. $\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} + \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{15} - \dfrac{1}{45}$ $v = 22.5\ \text{cm}$

- Find the power of a convex lens of focal length $25\ \text{cm}$.
  (a) $4\ \text{D}$  (b) $25\ \text{D}$  (c) $0.04\ \text{D}$  (d) $0.25\ \text{D}$
  Key: (a). Power of a lens is $P = \dfrac{1}{f}$ with $f$ in metres (dioptre). $f = 25\ \text{cm} = \dfrac{25}{100}\ \text{m}$ $P = \dfrac{1}{f} = 4\ \text{D}$

- Two point charges of $5\ \mu\text{C}$ and $6\ \mu\text{C}$ are placed $5\ \text{m}$ apart in air. Find the force between them. (Take $k = 9\times10^{9}\ \text{N m}^{2}\text{C}^{-2}$.)
  (a) $0.00108\ \text{N}$  (b) $0.0216\ \text{N}$  (c) $0.054\ \text{N}$  (d) $0.0108\ \text{N}$
  Key: (d). Coulomb's law gives the force between two point charges, $F = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q_1q_2}{r^{2}}$. $F = \dfrac{k q_1 q_2}{r^{2}} = \dfrac{9\times10^{9}\times5\times10^{-6}\times6\times10^{-6}}{5^{2}}$ $F = 0.0108\ \text{N}$

- A capacitor of capacitance $10\ \mu\text{F}$ is charged to a potential difference of $60\ \text{V}$. Calculate the energy stored in it.
  (a) $300\ \mu\text{J}$  (b) $36000\ \mu\text{J}$  (c) $18000\ \mu\text{J}$  (d) $600\ \mu\text{J}$
  Key: (c). The energy stored in a charged capacitor is $U = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^{2}$. $U = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}\times10\times60^{2}$ $U = 18000\ \mu\text{J}$

- Two capacitors of $4\ \mu\text{F}$ and $12\ \mu\text{F}$ are connected in series. Find the equivalent capacitance.
  (a) $0.333\ \mu\text{F}$  (b) $16\ \mu\text{F}$  (c) $3\ \mu\text{F}$  (d) $48\ \mu\text{F}$
  Key: (c). For capacitors in series $\dfrac{1}{C} = \dfrac{1}{C_1} + \dfrac{1}{C_2}$, so $C = \dfrac{C_1C_2}{C_1+C_2}$. $C = \dfrac{C_1C_2}{C_1+C_2} = \dfrac{4\times 12}{4+12}$ $C = 3\ \mu\text{F}$

- A potential difference of $24\ \text{V}$ is applied across a resistor of $8\ \Omega$. Find the current flowing through it.
  (a) $1.5\ \text{A}$  (b) $3\ \text{A}$  (c) $0.333\ \text{A}$  (d) $192\ \text{A}$
  Key: (b). Ohm's law states that the current through a conductor is proportional to the potential difference across it, $V = IR$. $I = \dfrac{V}{R} = \dfrac{24}{8}$ $I = 3\ \text{A}$

- Three resistors of $10\ \Omega$, $18\ \Omega$ and $10\ \Omega$ are connected in series. Calculate the equivalent resistance.
  (a) $1800\ \Omega$  (b) $3.91\ \Omega$  (c) $38\ \Omega$  (d) $12.7\ \Omega$
  Key: (c). For resistors in series the equivalent resistance is the sum, $R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$. $R_{eq} = R_1+R_2+R_3 = 10+18+10$ $R_{eq} = 38\ \Omega$

- Two resistors of $10\ \Omega$ and $18\ \Omega$ are connected in parallel. Find their equivalent resistance.
  (a) $180\ \Omega$  (b) $6.43\ \Omega$  (c) $14\ \Omega$  (d) $28\ \Omega$
  Key: (b). For resistors in parallel, $\dfrac{1}{R_{eq}} = \dfrac{1}{R_1} + \dfrac{1}{R_2}$. $\dfrac{1}{R_{eq}} = \dfrac{1}{10} + \dfrac{1}{18}$ $R_{eq} = 6.43\ \Omega$

- A device connected to a $24\ \text{V}$ supply draws a current of $6\ \text{A}$. Find the power consumed.
  (a) $30\ \text{W}$  (b) $4\ \text{W}$  (c) $144\ \text{W}$  (d) $72\ \text{W}$
  Key: (c). Electrical power is $P = VI$. $P = VI = 24\times 6$ $P = 144\ \text{W}$

- A cell of emf $12\ \text{V}$ and internal resistance $2\ \Omega$ is connected to an external resistance of $10\ \Omega$. Find the current in the circuit.
  (a) $1.5\ \text{A}$  (b) $1.2\ \text{A}$  (c) $1\ \text{A}$  (d) $6\ \text{A}$
  Key: (c). For a cell with internal resistance $I = \dfrac{E}{R + r}$. $I = \dfrac{E}{R+r} = \dfrac{12}{10+2}$ $I = 1\ \text{A}$

- A current of $5\ \text{A}$ flows through a resistor of $12\ \Omega$. Find the power dissipated as heat.
  (a) $720\ \text{W}$  (b) $300\ \text{W}$  (c) $120\ \text{W}$  (d) $60\ \text{W}$
  Key: (b). The power dissipated in a resistor is $P = I^{2}R$. $P = I^{2}R = 5^{2}\times12$ $P = 300\ \text{W}$

- A radioactive sample initially has $640$ nuclei and a half-life of $5\ \text{days}$. How many nuclei remain undecayed after $20\ \text{days}$?
  (a) $20$  (b) $320$  (c) $80$  (d) $40$
  Key: (d). After $n$ half-lives, $N = \dfrac{N_0}{2^{n}}$ with $n = \dfrac{t}{T_{1/2}}$. $n = \dfrac{t}{T_{1/2}} = \dfrac{20}{5} = 4$ $N = \dfrac{N_0}{2^{n}} = \dfrac{640}{2^{4}} = 40$

## bio11: 14 numeric templates

- A compound microscope has an objective lens of $10\times$ and an eyepiece of $10\times$. What is the total magnification?
  (a) $1000\ \times$  (b) $20\ \times$  (c) $100\ \times$  (d) $10\ \times$
  Key: (c). Total magnification equals the objective magnification multiplied by the eyepiece magnification. $M = M_{obj}\times M_{eye} = 10\times 10$ $M = 100\times$

- A cell measures $20\ \text{mm}$ in a drawing made at a magnification of $400\times$. What is its actual length in micrometres?
  (a) $50\ \mu\text{m}$  (b) $5\ \mu\text{m}$  (c) $100\ \mu\text{m}$  (d) $8\ \mu\text{m}$
  Key: (a). Actual size = image size / magnification, and $1\ \text{mm} = 1000\ \mu\text{m}$. Actual size $= \dfrac{20\ \text{mm}}{400} = \dfrac{20\times 1000\ \mu\text{m}}{400}$ Actual size $= 50\ \mu\text{m}$

- A cuboidal cell has a side length of $3\ \mu\text{m}$. What is its surface area to volume ratio?
  (a) $54\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$  (b) $2\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$  (c) $0.5\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$  (d) $18\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$
  Key: (b). For a cube of side $l$, surface area $=6l^2$ and volume $=l^3$, so SA:V $= 6/l$. SA $= 6l^2$, $V = l^3$ SA:V $= \dfrac{6}{3} = 2\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$

- An organism has $2n = 20$ chromosomes in its somatic cells. How many chromosomes does each gamete contain?
  (a) $10$  (b) $5$  (c) $40$  (d) $20$
  Key: (a). Meiosis halves the chromosome number, so a gamete is haploid ($n$). $n = \dfrac{2n}{2} = \dfrac{20}{2}$ $n = 10$

- A diploid cell has $2n = 10$ chromosomes. How many different chromosome combinations can its gametes carry through independent assortment (no crossing over)?
  (a) $5$  (b) $32$  (c) $25$  (d) $100$
  Key: (b). Independent assortment of $n$ chromosome pairs gives $2^n$ gamete combinations. $n = \dfrac{10}{2}$ pairs Combinations $= 2^n = 32$

  (a) $0.21$  (b) $0.42$  (c) $0.49$  (d) $0.09$
  Key: (b). Hardy-Weinberg: $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$ with $p = 1-q$; heterozygotes occur at $2pq$. $p = 1 - q = 1 - 0.3$ $2pq = 2\times p\times 0.3 = 0.42$

  Key: (b). The recessive phenotype is the genotype $aa$, with frequency $q^2$. $q^2 = 0.2^2$ Number $= q^2\times 1000 = 40$

  Key: (a). The recessive phenotype frequency equals $q^2$, so $q=\sqrt{q^2}$. $q^2 = \dfrac{16}{100}$ $q = \sqrt{q^2} = 0.4$

  (a) $13.9\ \text{min}$  (b) $6.93\ \text{min}$  (c) $20\ \text{min}$  (d) $27.8\ \text{min}$
  Key: (a). For a first-order reaction $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{0.693}{k}$. $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{0.693}{k} = \dfrac{0.693}{0.05}$ $t_{1/2} = 13.9\ \text{min}$

- Calculate the mass of copper deposited when a current of $3\ \text{A}$ is passed through $\text{CuSO}_4$ solution for $40$ minutes. (Cu $= 63.5$, $F = 96500\ \text{C mol}^{-1}$.)
  (a) $4.74\ \text{g}$  (b) $0.0395\ \text{g}$  (c) $2.37\ \text{g}$  (d) $1.58\ \text{g}$
  Key: (c). Faraday's first law gives $m = \dfrac{M I t}{nF}$. $m = \dfrac{MIt}{nF} = \dfrac{63.5\times3\times40\times60}{2\times96500}$ $m = 2.37\ \text{g}$

- For a reaction $\Delta H = -40\ \text{kJ}$ and $\Delta S = 100\ \text{J K}^{-1}$ at $500\ \text{K}$. Calculate $\Delta G$.
  (a) $-180\ \text{kJ}$  (b) $-90\ \text{kJ}$  (c) $-40.1\ \text{kJ}$  (d) $-50\ \text{kJ}$
  Key: (b). The Gibbs energy change is $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$ (convert $\Delta S$ to kJ). $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S = -40 - 500\times\dfrac{100}{1000}$ $\Delta G = -90\ \text{kJ}$

- Calculate the number of moles in $50\ \text{g}$ of a substance whose molar mass is $58\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.
  (a) $0.862\ \text{mol}$  (b) $0.431\ \text{mol}$  (c) $1.72\ \text{mol}$  (d) $1.16\ \text{mol}$
  Key: (a). The amount of substance is $n = \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{molar mass}}$. $n = \dfrac{m}{M} = \dfrac{50}{58}$ $n = 0.862\ \text{mol}$
